Case study

A/B testing with Sweed AI, built into Workflows

Workflows is the canvas where dispensary marketers build automated campaigns: an audience, a delay, an email, a condition. I designed the A/B testing layer for it and the Sweed AI tools that live inside that layer.

Sweed AI is the AI layer of the whole platform: product recommendations at the register, dynamic customer segments, on-screen help for budtenders, and in marketing it drafts banners, emails and survey insights. In Workflows I designed how Sweed AI works inside an A/B test.

The idea is simple. AI is good at producing options and has no way of knowing which one will sell. So Sweed AI drafts, tunes and checks the variants, and the test decides.

01

Sweed AI drafts, the test decides

Sweed AI sits inside the test, not in front of it. Every suggestion is labeled and reversible, and nothing goes out until the marketer starts the test.

  • Tunes the setup: suggests test share, metric and duration from the segment size and the campaign goal, and says why
  • Drafts variants: the marketer describes the goal, AI writes subject lines and content from the store catalog, active promos and each customer's past purchases
  • Checks compliance: every draft is checked against cannabis advertising rules before it can join the test, with a one-click rewrite
  • Explains the result: after the test, AI says why the winner won and what to test next

02

One email and a guess

A step in Workflows could send only one version of an email. Marketers had no way to compare subject lines or content, so campaign performance was unpredictable and hard to improve.

  • Built in: testing had to live inside the flow builder, with no redirects and no external tools
  • Flexible: marketers needed control over test size, the winning metric and how the winner is chosen
  • Light: setup and monitoring had to feel native and usable by any team, not only by analysts

03

A test is a property of the step

Instead of a separate tool, A/B testing became a checkbox on the email step. Turning it on opens the settings right in the side panel: the share of the segment that gets the test, the winning metric such as order placed rate, the test duration, and what the rest of the audience receives.

Variants sit in a table below the settings, and the canvas marks the step as a test, so it reads at a glance even in a long flow.

Across the platform

Designing the Admin Portal End-to-End Across All Product Streams

In Sweed Portal I worked across the entire admin platform, not a single vertical. I was responsible for the UX and cross-stream consistency of key product modules.

Even though my formal title was Senior Product Designer, the scope of responsibility and impact was closer to a Staff level role: I connected multiple teams, unified interaction patterns, aligned product logic between modules and ensured a seamless, predictable UX across the whole portal.

05

E-commerce / Marketing

A small UX ambiguity can translate into lost revenue or misaligned offers on the storefront.

E-commerce runs the online storefront operation: Active orders as a board and as a table, order packing with barcode verification, Dispatch on a map with drivers and alerts, and delivery shifts. Marketing sits right next to it with Events, Discounts, Segments and User Workflows: campaigns, promotions and loyalty mechanics. Together they are the growth layer of the platform.

I refined the configuration flows, aligned delivery and assignment logic with store-level settings and improved tools for non-technical staff, and made marketing and e-commerce feel like one system. A big part of the work was reducing hidden states and clarifying cause and effect. Order packing, for example, shows which items are verified and which are missing before the final Verified or Not verified decision, so staff never have to guess whether an order is ready.

A small UX ambiguity here can mean lost revenue or misaligned offers, so I unified the logic behind these controls and exposed key dependencies, making configuration predictable rather than accidental.

06

Customers

This module needed clarity, predictability and robustness more than visual novelty.

The Customers module is the foundation of the portal’s buyer management and patient verification. Dispensary staff start from the customer queue and open a profile with tabs for Info, Documents, Forms, Purchase history and Logs, to validate identities, maintain medical records, connect caregivers to patients and run compliance checks before a sale.

I extended and matured the existing UX: flows for validation, documents and medical eligibility, clearer states and edge cases, simpler navigation for high-frequency tasks. Because the module affects checkout and compliance risk, it needed clarity and robustness more than visual novelty.

A large part of the work was shaping the system rules behind customer states: when eligibility changes, which validations block, how caregiver relationships cascade. I defined edge scenarios with product and engineering to prevent loopholes that could lead to regulatory violations or checkout failure.

07

Store Setup

A single wrong setting can change pricing, eligibility or fulfillment.

Store Setup is the configuration layer of the portal: Products, Prices, Attributes, Categories, Brands, Distributors, Locations, Employees, Hardware and more. It defines what the business looks like operationally before any sales happen.

I evolved the UX for these foundational settings: navigation hierarchy, dependencies between settings, easier configuration without breaking store logic. A single wrong setting can change pricing, eligibility or fulfillment, so the UX had to reinforce safety and clarity. Category settings, for instance, decide which product fields are enabled (quality line, strain, scents, flavorings, potency), so they are grouped and labeled to be read before they are changed.

Much of the work was modeling the logic behind settings: what is global vs store-level, how controls are grouped, what stays in advanced menus. I reduced cognitive load while keeping full configurability for power users through defaults and progressive disclosure.

08

Settings

The goal was to make platform-wide decisions safer, not just easier to click through.

Settings is the infrastructure layer of the portal: E-commerce rules such as auto-approving orders and how long pickup, curbside and drive-through orders are held, Integrations like SendGrid, the store Schedule and Store behavior around promos, prices and refunds, plus Delivery, Notifications, Payment Methods, Company Profile and Billing. I supported its growing complexity while keeping a clean, predictable UX model.

I untangled technical concepts into understandable controls, grouped scattered toggles and made sensitive changes traceable and safe to edit. I set clear boundaries between organization-level and store-level logic, rethinking how inheritance, overrides and defaults are shown, so changes have a predictable scope.

Design changes here have long-term architectural consequences, so the work required systems thinking: configuration that is auditable, reversible when possible and resistant to misconfiguration. The goal was to make platform-wide decisions safer, not just easier to click through.

09

Dashboard / Reports / Money / Inventory

Rather than designing one-off pages, I worked at the system level.

These are the core operational modules of the portal that store managers and back-office teams use every day. Dashboard shows at a glance how the store and its campaigns are performing. Reports is where analysts build and save their own views, such as gross sales by day. Money controls cash: shifts, cash drawers and the gap between expected and actual amounts. Inventory covers purchases, counts and transfers between locations.

When I joined, these areas were already live and growing fast across several product streams. I refined and extended the existing UX and kept the modules consistent as new features arrived. Much of it came down to data hierarchy and edge cases: a report builder with metrics, intervals, filters and grouping side by side, a Shifts list where every difference between expected and actual cash is visible at a glance, and Purchases and Transfers with statuses that are easy to scan.

Rather than designing one-off pages, I worked at the system level, so each change fit a cohesive logic shared across the portal.
